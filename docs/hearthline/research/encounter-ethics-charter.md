# EncounterEthicsCharter Research Contract

## Primary promise

Enable an adult to privately author and selectively share a personal statement
of current encounter principles without converting the statement into a
contract, public badge, discovery input, moral score, consent record, or
platform-enforced compliance artifact.

## Object constraints

- The charter is private by default.
- Each section is optional and independently shareable.
- A recipient sees a section only after the author chooses the recipient,
  chooses the section, and the recipient accepts access.
- Every share grant has an expiry and can be revoked by the author.
- Revision revokes active grants so that changed values are not silently shared.
- Charter text cannot be public, searchable, indexed, embedded, ranked,
  recommended, advertised, analyzed, or included in model training.
- Charter text cannot be used for eligibility, reputation, attractiveness,
  compatibility, moderation scoring, or user classification.
- The charter is not a legal agreement, consent receipt, health claim,
  relationship obligation, or guarantee of future conduct.
- The charter includes no payment, gift-conditioned, transport-conditioned,
  housing-conditioned, debt-conditioned, or other value-for-intimacy pathway.
- The user can delete their charter and revoke access at any time.

## Always-stop conditions

- A user reports that the charter felt like a contract, moral test, badge,
  requirement, score, or proof of consent.
- Charter content or derived semantic data reaches discovery, ranking,
  recommendation, advertising, analytics, search, model training, moderation
  scoring, or person-level classification.
- A user feels compelled to author or share a charter to be treated as worthy
  of contact or to access a feature.
- A recipient can access content after an author revokes the relevant grant
  through a Hearthline product path.
- Payment, gifts, lodging, transportation, debt, work, goods, or other value
  becomes conditioned on intimacy through charter content or flow.
- An accessibility-equivalent author, share, read, revoke, and delete path is
  unavailable.
- The charter is used to pressure a user toward disclosure, a meeting, touch,
  intimacy, aftercare, or continued contact.

## Permitted measures

- Explicit participant feedback about clarity, agency, and pressure.
- Aggregate authoring, share-proposal, acceptance, decline, revision,
  revocation, expiry, and deletion counts.
- Accessibility task-completion outcomes using synthetic charter text.
- Security test outcomes demonstrating no content reaches unintended systems.

## Prohibited measures

- Charter quality score.
- Ethics score.
- Profile ranking.
- Compatibility ranking.
- Semantic sentiment analysis.
- Value, identity, health, recovery, financial, or vulnerability inference.
- Content-derived advertising, personalization, or recommendation.
