# Arizona Dating Services and Fraud-Prevention Notices

Status: Proposed public policy, subject to legal review and implementation verification.

Hearthline supports adult connection with privacy, consent, accessibility,
and member control. Those commitments must operate alongside applicable
consumer-protection requirements.

Arizona’s Dating Services law, A.R.S. § 44-7151 and following sections,
includes provisions concerning dating-service contracts, cancellation,
prohibited practices, and certain fraud-ban notices.

These requirements do not necessarily apply identically to every feature
or business model. Hearthline must review its actual services and applicable
law before publishing a final compliance policy.

## Why some notices cannot be fully generic

Hearthline ordinarily avoids notifications that reveal another member’s
identity, private activity, or sharing choices.

Arizona’s fraud-ban disclosure provision, A.R.S. § 44-7157, can require a
more specific notice.

Where the statutory conditions apply, the service must notify qualifying
Arizona members whom it knows previously received and responded to an
on-site message from a member banned for fraud.

The required notice includes:

- A username, identification number, or other identifier for the banned profile.
- A warning that the member may have used a false identity or may attempt fraud.
- A warning not to send money or personal financial information to another member.
- A link to information about avoiding online fraud.

The law specifies delivery timing and requires a clear and conspicuous
notice using a communication method consented to by the recipient.

A legally required fraud notice is different from a notification about
someone withdrawing consent, changing a boundary, or blocking contact.

## Our proposed privacy boundary

Any required fraud-notice process should use only the information necessary
to determine whether notice is required and to deliver it correctly.

It should not create:

- A public fraud badge or reputation score.
- A matching or discovery penalty based on report history.
- A general-purpose database of private conversations.
- Broad access to safety cases by unrelated services.
- Notifications revealing who submitted a report.
- An assumption that every report, block, technical restriction, or account
  closure is a statutory fraud ban.

A report requests review. It is not automatically a finding or a ban.

## Records and retention

Some applicable requirements may require contract records, qualifying
interaction information, or evidence of notice delivery.

Those records should have a documented purpose, restricted access,
a reviewed retention period, and a deletion process consistent with
applicable obligations.

They should not be reused for advertising, person-level scoring,
behavioral profiling, or candidate ranking.

Hearthline should not promise “no records retained” where a legal or
operational obligation requires specific records.

## Member control and support

A required fraud notice does not ask a member to confront another person,
continue communication, investigate, or send additional information.

Members should retain independent access to:

- Block and mute controls.
- Safety reporting.
- Privacy and consent settings.
- Support information.
- Review and correction pathways.
- Account closure and deletion requests, subject to disclosed retention limits.

No notice or accepted user agreement creates an obligation to reply, meet,
share personal information, or continue a connection.

## What this document does not establish

This draft does not establish that:

- Every Hearthline feature is covered by every provision of Chapter 28.
- A 30+ membership restriction is authorized by the Dating Services law.
- A particular interaction constitutes a crime.
- A platform notice guarantees protection from fraud.
- The described controls are already implemented.

The final policy must reflect current legal review and verified product behavior.

Hearthline’s ordinary discretion rules and any required legal disclosures
must be reconciled explicitly—not through hidden exceptions or unrestricted
sharing between services.
