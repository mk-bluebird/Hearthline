# Community Climate Signal Policy

## Purpose

Community Climate Signals measure whether a Hearthline product surface supports
clarity, autonomy, accessibility, privacy, and voluntary connection.

They evaluate product behavior in aggregate. They must not evaluate, rank,
classify, reward, punish, or profile individual members.

## Prohibited person-level outputs

Hearthline must not create, display, store, or use:

- Social acceptance score.
- Helpfulness score.
- Consent score.
- Safety score.
- Trust score.
- Reputation score.
- Popularity score.
- Desirability score.
- Risk score.
- Harassment likelihood score.
- Cooperation score.
- Community contribution score.
- Relationship-worth score.
- “Good member” label.
- Public or private leaderboard.

## Permitted aggregate signals

A signal may be collected only when it is:

1. Necessary to evaluate a specific product surface.
2. Aggregated across a sufficiently large population threshold.
3. Delayed so it cannot reveal live activity or small-group behavior.
4. Content-minimized.
5. Unable to identify a member, counterpart, venue, or protected class.
6. Documented with purpose, retention, deletion, and access controls.
7. Not used to affect an individual's visibility, access, moderation, or
   interaction opportunities.

Permitted categories:

- Explanation comprehension.
- Discovery-control discoverability.
- Consent-checkpoint completion clarity.
- Revocation propagation success.
- Keyboard path completion.
- Screen-reader path completion.
- Text-first path completion.
- Reduced-motion path completion.
- Low-bandwidth path completion.
- Report and appeal path reachability.
- Aggregate interface-confusion reports.
- Aggregate feature pause or hide selection.

## Forbidden signals

Do not collect, derive, retain, or analyze:

- Reply rate.
- Like/reaction count.
- Match count.
- Date count.
- Time spent.
- Message volume.
- Sexual-topic discussion.
- Intimate media sharing.
- Venue selection.
- Location disclosure.
- Identity disclosure.
- Protected-class membership.
- Health, recovery, disability, or financial context.
- Report count associated with an individual.
- Block count associated with an individual.
- Per-person engagement or retention.
- Network centrality.
- Profile popularity.
- “Acceptance” or “belonging” score.

## Non-retaliation

A member's use of pause, hide, block, report, withdrawal, or deletion controls
must not negatively affect discovery presentation, account status, feature
access, or future interaction opportunities.

## Human review

Aggregate signals may identify a product surface for design review. They must
not trigger automated sanctions against individuals.

Any material product change based on a Community Climate Signal requires review
by privacy, accessibility, safety, and member-rights owners.
